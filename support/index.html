Support

For an FM-1 running AISLING, FX-1 or anything else.

Keep a copy of your FM-1, change what it runs, and bring it back if it won't start.

01

The tool

What is M-Flasher?

A free program for Windows, macOS and Linux that reads and writes the FM-1's whole memory over USB, even when the firmware on it does not run.

It is by Julius Vitkauskas, not by AISLING, and it works with any firmware: M-VAVE's own, AISLING, FX-1 and the rest. AISLING's installer in the browser is still the short way to put AISLING on. M-Flasher is for what the installer cannot do: a backup of everything, going from one custom firmware to another, and an FM-1 the installer can no longer see.

Before it writes anything it reads the whole memory, checks that the package is for this model and this unit, saves a backup, and shows what would change. After writing, it checks what it wrote.

Get M-Flasher Its own guide

Both open Julius Vitkauskas's pages. His guide is the full one, and the one to trust where it and this page differ.

02

The everyday use

Back up, then switch firmware

Six steps. The backup comes first, every time: it is the only thing that brings back the sounds and saves a firmware kept.

  1. Install itOn a Mac, drag M-Flasher to Applications. It is not signed by Apple, so the first time: System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.
  2. Plug the FM-1 inStraight into the computer with a USB data cable, not through a hub, and switched on. Close anything else that uses MIDI.
  3. Download modePress DOWNLOAD MODE in M-Flasher. The FM-1's screen stays as it was. A Mac asks for your password, then whether M-Flasher may use a removable volume: Allow.
  4. Back upBACKUP FULL FLASH. It saves a 1 MiB .bin and a .json beside it in Documents/M-Flasher/backups. Keep both.
  5. Choose the firmwareFLASH FIRMWARE, then the .fwsc package: AISLING's, FX-1's or M-VAVE's, as downloaded. Then REVIEW & FLASH, and read what it says it will change.
  6. RestartWhen it has written and checked, unplug the FM-1, switch it off and on. It starts the new firmware.

AISLING's package FX-1's package M-VAVE's firmware

  • If DOWNLOAD MODE finds nothing and the FM-1 runs AISLING: hold OCT− and OCT+ together for five seconds, until the screen shows UBOOT. With m-bootloader (below), hold REC while switching on.
  • On a Mac, start M-Flasher soon after the FM-1 enters download mode. Left alone for about half a minute, the FM-1 may leave it again.
  • An install writes the firmware and nothing else. Settings and saves stay where they were, and each firmware keeps them in places of its own. To go back to a firmware with what you saved in it, restore the backup made while it was installed (RESTORE FULL FLASH).
  • A backup holds one FM-1's keys and calibration. Restored onto another FM-1, M-Flasher keeps that unit's own.

Tried here · An FM-1 running FX-1 1.1 was sent to download mode by the button, backed up, and given AISLING 1.4.1, from a Mac. It started AISLING. That FM-1 has the older memory chip (M-Flasher shows 0x856014 (old)). A second FM-1, with the newer chip (0xB36014 (new)), was backed up and given AISLING 1.5 from M-VAVE's firmware. It plays, and a save was still there after switching off and on.

Not tried here · Windows and Linux; restoring a backup; whether AISLING's saves are still there after a spell on FX-1.

03

Once per FM-1

Install m-bootloader

The bootloader is the first small program the FM-1 runs. Julius Vitkauskas's replaces M-VAVE's and adds a way into download mode from the FM-1's own keys.

With M-VAVE's bootloader, an FM-1 whose firmware does not start can only be reached with extra hardware, a Raspberry Pi Pico or a second instrument. With m-bootloader it is two fingers:

Held while switching onWhat happens
RECDownload mode, ready for M-Flasher.
PLAYA recovery menu: the firmware's state, Boot app, Download mode, Boot anyway, and a MIDI mode that a browser installer can write through.
nothingIt checks the firmware, then starts it. Damaged firmware opens the menu instead of being started.
  1. Back up firstSteps 1 to 4 above. The backup holds M-VAVE's bootloader too, so it is also the way back.
  2. Look at Flash chipUnder device info. 0x856014 (old): go on. 0xB36014 (new), in FM-1s sold from late 2026: m-bootloader is little tested there, its author says at your own risk, and M-Flasher warns.
  3. Flash bootloaderOn an FM-1 with M-VAVE's bootloader, M-Flasher offers this by itself once it has read the memory. Otherwise press FLASH BOOTLOADER. It has the build for the FM-1 inside it.
  4. Review and flashIt writes the first 16 KiB only, and keeps the chip key that firmware installers need. The firmware, settings and sounds are not touched.
  5. Try the keysUnplug, switch off, then on holding REC: M-Flasher should find the FM-1 again. Switch off and on with nothing held, and the firmware starts as before.
  • M-VAVE's own updates put M-VAVE's bootloader back. After one, install m-bootloader again.
  • To take it off: restore the backup, or FLASH BOOTLOADER with an official M-VAVE package chosen as the stock one.
  • AISLING and FX-1 need neither bootloader in particular. Their installers write the firmware and leave the bootloader as it is.

Tried here · AISLING 1.4.1 and FX-1 1.1 both start and play on an FM-1 with m-bootloader 2.4 (2.4-395e6cc, as M-Flasher reads it).

Not tried here · The install itself: that FM-1 already had m-bootloader, so the five steps above are from M-Flasher's guide, not from doing it. Nor the two keys, the menu, or MIDI mode with AISLING's installer.

04

When it goes wrong

The FM-1 won't start

A dark screen is nearly always the chip waiting in download mode, not a dead FM-1. In order:

  1. Off and onIf you sent it to download mode yourself, that is all it needs: the firmware starts again.
  2. Open M-FlasherPlug the FM-1 in and switch it on. An FM-1 whose firmware is damaged often goes to download mode by itself, and M-Flasher finds it.
  3. Hold RECWith m-bootloader: switch on holding REC. M-Flasher finds it.
  4. Put it backRESTORE FULL FLASH with your backup, or FLASH FIRMWARE with any package for the FM-1.

With M-VAVE's bootloader, no backup, and a Mac: AISLING's own rescue script puts M-VAVE's firmware back from the command line. It is in the manual, under Recovering an FM-1 that won't start.

If none of that reaches it, the chip has to be forced into download mode from outside. Below are two ways, neither tried here.

With a Raspberry Pi Pico

Before you start
  • Not tried here. This is one person's account of one FM-1. AISLING has not built or tested it.
  • The guide is written for a different board, the Seeed XIAO RP2040. The post names three differences from it: the board, the cable and no soldering. By that account the guide's own firmware file ran on a Pico; the post is the only evidence for that read here.
  • A newer unit may not be covered. The guide's example check shows the older memory chip (856014). FM-1s sold from late 2026 have another (B36014), and nothing read for this page says the route works on it.
  • It puts M-VAVE's V15 on, not what was there before. Install the firmware you want afterwards.
  • At your own risk. Neither AISLING nor M-VAVE supports it.

A post by u/M4XW3LL_X on r/MVaveFM1, Unbricked my FM-1 with a Raspberry Pi Pico, is the account this is taken from. The poster's FM-1 went black halfway through a flash of M-VAVE's V16 with M-UPGRADE, over Felucca. After that neither Windows nor Linux saw it, and no key combination or restart helped, though the charging light still worked. With a Pico and the FM-1 Transporter it started again, on V15. They describe themselves as not a hardware person.

What they used: an original Pico (RP2040) with its header pins on, a breadboard, jumper wires, and an old USB-A to USB-C data cable to cut up. No soldering.

Cable wireGoes to
Green (D+)GP0, the Pico's pin 1
White (D−)GP1, pin 2
Black (ground)GND, pin 3
Red (5 V)Nothing. Tape over its end. The FM-1 runs on its own battery.
  1. Charge the FM-1Fully, before anything else: it runs on its battery all the way through.
  2. Wire itCut the cable and strip the green, white and black wires. Put each in the same breadboard row as its pin, and hold it there by pushing a jumper wire's pin into the same hole. The USB-C end goes to the FM-1.
  3. Get V15The guide restores V15, and M-VAVE's download now gives V16. The Internet Archive's copy of V15 is the one AISLING's rescue script uses.
  4. Follow the guideQuixotic7's Transporter guide takes you from loading the board to a backup of the FM-1's memory, and writes V15 only after you type WRITE. Switch the FM-1 on when it says to.
  • A separate route: kurogedelic's FM-1 Transporter, which the guide's board program comes from, has a file built for the Pico (fm1_transporter_pico.uf2, marked untested) and its own tools. Follow its instructions from start to finish if you take it.
  • Other Pico guides use other pins. Take the wiring and the program from the same place, and don't mix them.
  • Keep the backup. It holds that FM-1's own settings and data.

With thanks to u/M4XW3LL_X for the post; to Quixotic7 and kurogedelic for the tools; and to Rastapopolix, whom the post credits with showing that a Pico works. The account of what happened, the parts and which wire goes to GP0, GP1 and GND are the post's. The pin numbers are the Pico's own. The rest is from the tools' pages, read on 26 November 2026.

With a second FM-1, or an SMK-37

If you have, or can borrow, one that works, Julius Vitkauskas's M-Unbricker repairs the dead one over a USB-C cable. It gives it m-bootloader and erases its old firmware and settings, so install a firmware afterwards. AISLING isn't on its list of firmwares the working one can be running: M-VAVE's own V15 and V16 are. Check the list on its page, and put a listed one on first.

Not tried here · M-Unbricker. This is from its own page, read on 26 November 2026.

From M-Flasher's guide, in its words shortened.

The Mac calls my FM-1 something else a name left over from another device

M-Flasher's first line may show another instrument's name beside "running": here, an FM-1 was listed as an SMK-37 Elite. macOS remembers a MIDI device's name by its USB number and the socket it was in, and several custom firmwares share a number. It does no harm: once the FM-1 is in download mode, M-Flasher reads the model from the memory itself.

To clear the old name: Audio MIDI Setup → Window → Show MIDI Studio, unplug the FM-1, and delete the greyed-out device.

Messages on the FM-1's screen BAD CONFIG, FAILED −5, APP BAD CRC

These are m-bootloader's and M-VAVE's updater's, not AISLING's, and what they mean depends on the versions involved. M-Flasher's guide and m-bootloader's status notes list them. In short: BAD CONFIG is an older m-bootloader, and installing the current one from M-Flasher fixes it; APP BAD CRC, APP INCOMPLETE and APP NOT FOUND mean the firmware is damaged or missing, so install it again.

Windows says it is a threat Defender and unsigned programs

M-Flasher is not signed, so Windows may say it protected your PC (More info → Run anyway), and Microsoft Defender sometimes removes a new release as a guess. Its author reports each one to Microsoft, and his guide says what to do meanwhile. Download it only from his page or his releases on GitHub.
